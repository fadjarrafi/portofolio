Most of the feature requests I work on don't come from other developers.

## Requests That Made Me Think

They come from sales, admin, HR, legal, marketing, even finance. Nobody talks in technical terms. No one says "we need a new endpoint" or "this needs a state management refactor." What I get instead is "can we export this report every month-end?" or "customers are complaining approvals take too long, can we speed it up?"

At first I just translated those requests straight into technical tasks without asking why. Until one day I got stuck trying to prioritize which feature to build first, even though technically they all looked equally important.

## Learning from a Friend

That's when I started talking more with a friend of mine who works as a digital marketer. They're the one who taught me how business actually makes decisions: ROI, funnels, retention, and why one small feature can matter more than a big one if it directly affects revenue or another team's efficiency.

Turns out a lot of business decisions aren't about what's technically cool, but about a trade-off you can actually measure.

## Why This Matters

Once I understood that, how I define requirements changed. I started asking "what problem is this actually solving?" before writing a single line of code. The technical decisions I make now line up better with what the business actually needs. It's not just about writing efficient code anymore, it's about being efficient for the business too.

Understanding business language doesn't mean a developer has to become a marketing or finance expert. But at minimum, it's enough to translate their language into a technical solution that actually connects to what they need.
